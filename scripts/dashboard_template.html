How concentrated is it?

HHI adds up the squared market shares of every partner (0–10,000). Higher means more dependence on a few countries; competition authorities treat 1,500+ as "moderately concentrated".

How to read it: each line is one country's share of Egypt's total imports (or exports, with the toggle). The five largest partners over the whole period are shown. A rising line means Egypt relies more on that country.

Q6 · Customs mirror checkRisk

Partners record more imports from Egypt than Egypt records as exports

Every shipment is declared twice: by the exporting country (valued FOB, at the port of loading) and by the importing country (valued CIF, including freight and insurance). Comparing the two "mirror" records is a standard customs-risk technique for spotting under-declaration, misrouted goods and classification errors.

What "normal" looks like

Imports should sit slightly above 1.0×, because Egypt's CIF value includes freight that the partner's FOB value does not. They mostly do.

Exports should sit slightly below 1.0× (about 0.90–0.95×) for the same reason. They sit lower, at in most years. That suggests part of Egypt's exports is not captured in its own records.

How to read it: each line is Egypt's recorded value divided by what its partners recorded for the same trade (same partners, same year). 1.0× means the two sides agree exactly. The shaded band is the normal range for imports once freight and insurance are counted.

Largest disagreements in 2023 (flows of $1bn or more)

Likely causes, which this data cannot confirm: energy exporters often don't report destinations for petroleum and pipeline gas (Kuwait, Israel), and Gulf re-export hubs blur the country of origin (UAE). Each case needs a product-level mirror check.

About the data

Where the numbers come from and how they were processed

UN Comtrade is the United Nations database of official international trade statistics. Each country's customs authority reports what crossed its border, by product, partner country and year. This dashboard uses Egypt's own reports (reporter code 818) plus every other country's reports of trade with Egypt.

Glossary

HS code
Harmonized System: the worldwide customs product code. 2 digits give a chapter (27 = mineral fuels), 4 digits a heading (2711 = petroleum gases).
CIF
Cost, Insurance & Freight. How importers value goods: the price plus shipping to the border.
FOB
Free On Board. How exporters value goods: the price at the port of loading, without shipping.
Mirror data
The same trade recorded by the partner country. Comparing both sides reveals reporting gaps.
HHI
Herfindahl-Hirschman Index of partner concentration, 0–10,000.
Bunkers
Fuel sold to ships and aircraft. Comtrade records it as a partner, not a country.
"nes"
"Not elsewhere specified": trade whose partner country was not identified.

Limitations

  • Nominal US dollars. Values are not adjusted for inflation or for the Egyptian pound's exchange rate.
  • Goods only. Services such as Suez Canal fees, tourism and remittances are not in Comtrade.
  • Late reporters. Mirror data for 2024–2025 covers fewer partners, because some countries file late. 2023 is the most complete recent year.
  • Weights unreliable. Within a year, every wheat origin carries the same USD per tonne, so the weights are estimates. Unit prices are not analysed.
  • Non-country buckets. 0.5–3.3% of trade each year goes to Bunkers, Free Zones or unidentified areas. It counts in totals but is left out of partner rankings.
